# Phase 8: extras walkthrough

## 8.1 Privacy blur ([blur.cpp](../../engine/src/blur/blur.cpp))

`rearview export <event id> --blur` cuts the event clip, then sends `blur {path, out_path}` to an engine started with `--lazy` (models load only for this job).

- **Faces**: OpenCV's YuNet (`face_detection_yunet_2023mar.onnx`, MIT, from the OpenCV model zoo) through `cv::FaceDetectorYN`, run on a 1280-wide copy of each frame for speed, score threshold 0.6.
- **Number plates: no plate model.** The plan's "check the licence before choosing one" step found no plate detector with a clear licence and an ONNX export that works at this resolution. Instead every vehicle box from YOLOX (car, motorcycle, bus, truck) gets its lower-middle band blurred (25–75% of the width, 55–95% of the height), which is where a plate sits on a vehicle seen from ahead or behind. This over-blurs (bumpers, grilles) but cannot miss a plate on a detected vehicle. A real plate detector can replace `plate_regions` later.
- **Heads**: YuNet misses small, distant faces, so the top 22% of every person box is blurred too.
- **No flicker**: `RegionHold` keeps each region for 8 frames after it was last seen (matched by IoU > 0.2), so a detector missing one frame doesn't expose the face or plate for that frame.
- Every box grows by 15% on each side, then is blurred by downscaling ×8, a Gaussian, and upscaling: strong enough that text can't be read, with a cost that doesn't grow with box size.
- Output is re-encoded (MPEG-4 Part 2 through OpenCV) and recorded in `clips` with `blurred = 1`.
- Tests ([blur_test.cpp](../../engine/tests/blur_test.cpp)): plate and head regions from boxes; expansion clipped to the frame; black text on white is destroyed (no near-black pixel left) while pixels outside the box are untouched; the hold keeps a region through 3 missed frames and drops it after; YuNet loads and finds no face in a blank 1440p frame.
- **Input needed**: a frame-by-frame check of 5 exported clips with plates and pedestrians (task 8.1 "Done when").

The camera's own overlay (speed, GPS, **own plate DJYR 963**, time) is burnt into every frame; it isn't blurred by this command. Crop or cover it before publishing.

## 8.2 Driving score ([score.go](../../go/internal/report/score.go))

`rearview trips --score` scores every trip, stores `trips.score`, prints each trip's breakdown and a weekly trend (distance-weighted average per ISO week and a one-line bar chart).

- Points lost per event (config `score`): hard brake 4, swerve 3, near-miss 10, following close 2 per minute, tailgated 0 (being tailgated isn't the driver's doing).
- Scaled per 100 km: points × 100 ÷ max(distance, 5 km). The 5 km floor stops a 1 km trip with one brake from losing 400 points.
- Score = 100 − sum of the breakdown. When that would go below 0, a final "floor" line with negative points is added so the breakdown still adds up exactly (the "Done when").
- Tests: clean trip, mixed events at 100 km, scaling at 50 km, a short trip hitting the floor, tailgated free, following minutes from `details_json`, weekly averages and the bar chart.

## 8.3 Incident report PDF ([pdf.go](../../go/internal/report/pdf.go))

`rearview report <event id>` writes one A4 page next to the event's clip:

- Header: type, local and UTC time; a table with score, source, camera, coordinates, trip, recording file and offset, details, clip file.
- Three key frames (2 s before, at, and 2 s after the event) from the engine's new `extract_frames` command, which seeks and saves JPEGs.
- A graph of the 30 s around the event: speed as a thick solid line on the left axis, longitudinal G as a thin dashed line on the right axis, a dotted vertical line at the event, grid and legend. The camera has no accelerometer (task 1.2), so G is Δspeed/Δt from the 1 Hz GPS (a hard brake of 10 km/h in a second shows as −0.28 g).
- Black and white only: line weight and dash pattern, not colour, tell the two series apart.
- Tests: G derivation on a synthetic brake; a PDF is produced with frames and graph.
